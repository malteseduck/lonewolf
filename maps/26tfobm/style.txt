Repaint this map of the Kingdom of Bor and its surrounding territories as a richly detailed hand-painted fantasy illustration on aged parchment, a dwarven realm of towering stone around one long inland water. Most of the land is mountain: continuous sculpted, shaded three-dimensional ranges of warm orange-brown rock, never rows of identical symbols. Only the high peaks at the heart of Bor, around Boradon, carry cold blue-white caps; the other ranges stay bare rock.
The hills are low shaded rises on warm tan ground, in the west and south, never green and never desert sand. The plains are open green grassland. The coniferous forests are dense dark green firs on their green ground, only where the map draws them. The swamp along the eastern edge is pale mauve marsh ground with reeds and small pools, deepening to dark plum at its heart, never green. The water is a clear cold blue with soft depth and wave texture, never tropical turquoise; the rivers are narrow and blue. The roads and tracks stay pale and dashed exactly where they are, and every castle, tower and house stays as drawn.
Keep, each in its place with its own colours and every word and number in it legible, enriched with finer detail: the title panel, the star-patterned scale panel with its numbers, the cross-shaped compass with its letters, the key with its swatches and names. Add finer detail in the existing frame, with its red and blue bars, its white knotwork and its six shields: the crowns at the top, the crossed axes and the anvil at the sides, the tools at the bottom.
No ships or creatures, no armies, no leafy trees or farmland, no snow outside the central peaks, no new castles, towns, bridges, mines or ruins.
